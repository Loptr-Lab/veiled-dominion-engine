Class 0: Is Loptr Lab right for you?

For prospective contributors, collaborators, partners and clients. Allow about 30 minutes for the exercise after reading; take longer or work asynchronously. Outcome: a short mutual-fit brief and one next decision. This is a self-check, not a qualification or application form.

We are an accessibility-first, people-over-profit studio building in public. We start with the resources already available and make small, evidence-led spending decisions. Before adding an expense, compare its expected benefit, uncertainty and time to return with current operating costs, existing debt obligations and available cash. Reuse existing assets first; spend only within an affordable, bounded test, then review actual costs and results before expanding. Expected revenue is a planning assumption, not cash available to spend. Open contributions are voluntary and unpaid; paid services require a separate signed scope and confirmed funding. No future pay, employment, revenue, funding or launch date is promised.

Make a one-page fit brief

Keep answers in your own notebook, text document, private audio note or sketch. Nothing is collected, graded or sent by this page.

  1. Your need: What outcome do you need, for whom, and by when? Are you seeking learning, public contribution, a paid service or a partnership?
  2. Your resources: List existing skills, tools, permitted assets and realistically available hours. Identify access barriers and what support would help. Do not disclose finances or health records publicly.
  3. Our mutual value: What can each party actually provide? Name missing expertise, cash-dependent work and assumptions that need confirmation.
  4. One small test: Define one reversible deliverable, its time cap, observable success condition and review date. Name what remains outside scope.
  5. Boundaries: Specify what may be public, attribution preferences, rights questions, compensation expectations and a safe stopping point. Ask who can approve the work.
  6. Evidence: What result would make you continue, revise or stop? Separate attention, repeated use, willingness to pay and actual net receipts.

Worked studio example: Rebirth

Prepare one readable on-site episode using the existing origin draft and existing artwork selected for review against the approved character specification. Record actual editing and accessibility work time; identify missing panels before spending. Ask readers where the story becomes unclear and whether they choose to return.

Episode engagement informs the next bounded story decision. It does not prove game demand or ROI. Any Veiled Dominion test remains separately scoped. Packaged manga downloads remain withheld pending review of the agreed audience goals and a separate owner decision to release. The 90-day story test targets 200 distinct episode-one starters, 100 completions, 40 returning readers for a later episode and 20 substantive responses from distinct people. These are planning targets, not achieved results or proof of ROI. Game participation is measured separately. No counter or automatic unlock is implemented here. Ordinary reader-note exports elsewhere in The Threshold remain available.

Choose an honest outcome

  • Explore together: Needs and resources appear compatible. Discuss one bounded task through the existing contributor route; agree terms before work starts.
  • Clarify first: Funding, access, rights, time or expectations remain unresolved. Name the blocker and responsible decision-maker; do not imply work has been commissioned.
  • Another route fits better: You need guaranteed income, private production capacity, urgent delivery or services we cannot currently supply. Declining is a valid outcome.

Completion does not establish membership, production access, employment, ownership or canon authority. Financial support never buys acceptance. Both parties may stop.
