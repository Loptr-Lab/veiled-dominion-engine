# Decision brief: make independent creative work more repeatable

**For:** independent publishers, accessibility advocates, game-development organizations, educators, funders and platform teams.  
**From:** Loptr Lab — research and applied-learning program.  
**Status:** case study prepared for independent review; no partnership, endorsement or funding commitment is implied.

## The proposition

Independent creators can produce ambitious, culturally valuable work. The industry has not made the conditions that support that work—discovery, publishing capacity, accessibility research, distribution and sustainable economics—reliably available to the next creator.

HyperDot, developed by Tribe Games and published by GLITCH, is a useful Minnesota case for examining how local community, independent publishing, platform opportunities and disabled-player research contributed to one project's path.

## What is documented

- MinneBar 14's April 27, 2019 schedule listed HyperDot in its local indie arcade.
- Tribe Games identifies Charles McGregor as a one-person Minnesota studio.
- HyperDot launched January 31, 2020 with GLITCH credited as publisher.
- The creator's accessibility account describes a 2019 #HyperDotA11y research effort with disabled content creators.
- IGDA's 2019 developer survey documented concerns around discovery, funding and working conditions among respondents.

## What we do not claim

We do not know the project's development budget, lifetime sales, net profit, creator income, GLITCH's exact financial contribution, or the user's attendance at the listed event. A strong public reception is not proof of financial sustainability. Comparisons with A Short Hike and Celeste are about observable scope and creative model, not equivalent budgets.

## The gap

A solo creator may be expected to perform the work of a studio and publisher: production, accessibility, testing, marketing, distribution, business operations and audience development. These demands create structural disadvantages even when the work itself is excellent.

## The experiment

PLAY IT FORWARD Experiment 02 tests a small creator-support pathway using existing assets and existing channels. It records:
- baseline reach and channel performance before intervention;
- asset rights and provenance;
- accessibility and usability barriers;
- creator hours and direct costs;
- discovery and conversion events;
- deliverables, reuse and actual revenue, if any;
- failures and limits.

No new platform or account is a default requirement. The experiment proceeds only where existing assets, permissions and audience justify the work.

## The request

We are seeking bounded review, not a general endorsement:
1. **Creator / publisher:** correct the historical account and identify the support that mattered.
2. **Accessibility specialists and disabled players:** challenge the access claims and review our test method.
3. **Industry researchers:** validate the economic interpretation and point to stronger or newer evidence.
4. **Funders / educators / platform teams:** assess whether the proposed experiment has a credible, measurable public benefit.

## What a useful decision looks like

A reviewer can identify a factual error, improve the methodology, name a missing cost, recommend a better measure, or decide that the proposed experiment is not worth pursuing. A negative or null finding is a legitimate outcome.
