LOPTR LAB / DECISION BRIEF

Make independent creative work more repeatable

A research proposition for publishers, accessibility advocates, educators, funders and platform teams.

The proposition

Independent creators can produce ambitious, culturally valuable work. The industry has not made the conditions that support that work—discovery, publishing capacity, accessibility research, distribution and sustainable economics—reliably available to the next creator.

What is documented

  • MinneBar 14's April 27, 2019 schedule listed HyperDot in its local indie arcade.
  • Tribe Games identifies Charles McGregor as a one-person Minnesota studio.
  • HyperDot launched January 31, 2020 with GLITCH credited as publisher.
  • The creator's accessibility account describes a 2019 #HyperDotA11y research effort with disabled content creators.
  • IGDA's 2019 developer survey documented concerns around discovery, funding and working conditions among respondents.

What we do not claim

We do not know the game's development budget, lifetime sales, net profit, creator income, GLITCH's exact financial contribution, or the user's attendance at the listed event. Public reception does not prove financial sustainability. Comparisons with A Short Hike and Celeste concern observable scope and creative model, not equivalent budgets.

The experiment

PLAY IT FORWARD Experiment 02 tests a small creator-support pathway using existing assets and existing channels. It records baseline reach, rights, accessibility, creator hours, direct costs, discovery actions, deliverables and actual revenue if available. No new platform or account is a default requirement.

Review the experiment protocol

The request

  1. Creator / publisher: correct the historical account and identify the support that mattered.
  2. Accessibility specialists and disabled players: challenge the access claims and review the test method.
  3. Industry researchers: validate the economic interpretation and identify stronger current evidence.
  4. Funders / educators / platform teams: assess whether the proposed experiment has a credible, measurable public benefit.

What a useful decision looks like

A reviewer identifies a factual error, improves the methodology, names a missing cost, recommends a better measure, or concludes that the experiment is not worth pursuing. A negative or null finding is legitimate.
