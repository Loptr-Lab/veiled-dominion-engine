Public-interest Reviewer Map

Find the right expertise. Ask a bounded question. Record corrections transparently.

Purpose and boundary

This map identifies people and organizations whose public professional remit makes them appropriate to review the HyperDot case study or its proposed experiment. It is not a private dossier. No personal contact details, vulnerabilities, private relationships, speculative allegations or coercive “leverage” belong in this public repository. Inclusion is not endorsement.

Review priorities

  1. Charles McGregor / Tribe Games: correct the development timeline, GLITCH support description, accessibility history and unanswered economics questions. Not contacted.
  2. GLITCH / historical Minnesota leadership: clarify the support model, responsibilities, resources, terms that can be disclosed and what was repeatable. Not contacted.
  3. IGDA Game Accessibility SIG: challenge the accessibility interpretation and recommend evaluation criteria. Not contacted.
  4. AbleGamers: assess whether disabled participants have meaningful influence and safe participation in the proposed test. Not contacted.
  5. IGDA advocacy / research leadership: validate survey interpretation and identify stronger current evidence. Not contacted.
  6. Independent publishers and small-studio operators: assess feasibility, cost and sustainability of shared support functions. Not contacted.
  7. Educators and university game programs: assess reproducibility and learning value. Not contacted.
  8. Platform accessibility and distribution teams: identify actionable platform-level improvements without implying a partnership. Not contacted.

Safe review sequence

  1. Ask the creator for factual correction.
  2. Ask the historical publisher/community source to distinguish documented support from interpretation.
  3. Ask accessibility specialists and disabled-player advocates to review the access account and test safeguards.
  4. Ask industry researchers to validate historical survey interpretation and point to newer evidence.
  5. Ask publishers, educators and platform teams targeted questions after the case is corrected.

Qualification and outreach contract

  • Verify current professional role and public affiliation.
  • Match the person's public experience to a specific review question.
  • Record publicly disclosed interests that could affect interpretation.
  • Make one bounded request; no bulk or automated outreach.
  • Do not request confidential contracts, private financial records or unpublished participant data.
  • Agree on attribution and quotation permission before publishing comments.
  • Review can be declined or withdrawn without penalty. Commissioned work requires a separate scope and compensation agreement.
  • Never imply endorsement without explicit permission.
