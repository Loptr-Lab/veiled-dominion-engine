FIELD LAB / EXPERIMENT 01

Turn existing footage into an adventure

Make the story from what you already have. The constraint is the creative engine.

Challenge

Use a small collection of existing, rights-cleared footage to pitch and produce a new short adventure. Do not begin by buying gear or generating more material. First find the story hidden in the footage.

Workflow

  1. Inventory. Create a shot list with filename, duration, subject, location (if known), audio, quality, permission/provenance, and notable moments. Don't upload private or uncleared material to a service.
  2. Analyze. Use a video-capable AI tool to describe observable content and propose clusters or story beats. Require timecodes and uncertainty; verify every observation against the actual clip.
  3. Pitch. Generate three story premises using only verified shots and a clear limitation (one location, no new dialogue, one character, etc.). Human selects and rewrites.
  4. Outline. Build a beginning, escalation, turn, and payoff. Mark gaps as footage needed, sound, title card, or optional synthetic insert.
  5. Storyboard. Use ScreenWeaver or another storyboard/screenwriting tool if available. Confirm current capabilities, privacy, cost, and export before sending assets. MCP connection is optional; no workflow depends on a single provider.
  6. Edit. Assemble a rough cut with existing footage. AI may help with transcripts, shot logging, ideas, or sound cleanup; the editor decides rhythm and meaning.
  7. Review. Check continuity, factual claims, likeness/voice permissions, music rights, generated-media disclosure, accessibility/captions, and final exports.

Prompt template

Act as a story editor, not the director. Analyze only the footage descriptions and timecodes I provide. Do not invent shots or claim you watched files you cannot access. Propose three short adventure concepts that can be made from these assets. For each: logline, verified usable shots, sequence, missing material, sound approach, and risks. Label assumptions and ask before adding new assets. Prioritize a strong story and achievable edit over expensive production.

Evaluation

  • Could a viewer follow the story without explanation?
  • Did AI save measurable time without reducing authorship?
  • Are every asset's rights and permissions documented?
  • Are invented or synthetic elements clearly identified?
  • What was spent, what was made, and what can be reused?
