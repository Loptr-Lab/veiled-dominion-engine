Optional maker pathway: game rules and physical interaction

Status: proposed, source-based learning pathway. The case studies are at different stages; neither is a verified hardware build or a demonstrated learner outcome.

Study two directions of translation: a pinball machine simulated in software, and turn-based Duet software considered for a physical LED chessboard. They differ in feedback tempo and access needs. Pinball demands real-time motor input and immediate state feedback; chess allows deliberate turns but must replace browser screen-reader feedback when moved to a standalone board.

Suggested sequence

  1. Read each project's provenance and boundaries. Observe an existing table or review the digital reference with permission where needed.
  2. For pinball, draft a shot/state/feedback map and test one accessible software interaction. Record what players actually understand; do not assert learning outcomes before playtests.
  3. For Duet, draw the software-to-board boundary and an audio-first state/announcement table. Compare a sample move against the duet-solo-hackathon multiplayer app's server-authoritative state and its M1 test plan before selecting hardware.
  4. Document an access check for both: controls, recovery from errors, non-color cues, and nonvisual state. Contrast real-time versus turn-based demands.
  5. Scope physical components, budget, maintenance, and maker supervision only after the relevant software and accessibility questions are answered.

This index is a pointer, not approval to build, a partnership with a venue or artist, a funding promise, or a transfer of project ownership. Corridors and Duet keep their own rules and release decisions. Generalized lessons can inform the game development and hybrid creative technology tracks; Veiled Dominion changes require its own engine review.

Read before you repair · iFixit

Start with the instrument you have. Record its model and visible condition without opening it; do not publish a serial number or a minor’s personal details. A general guide does not establish that your synthesizer or firmware is compatible.

  1. Read iFixit Device Safety.
  2. Use Identifying Major Electronic Components as a reading activity: name three components and their functions from the diagrams, without opening or powering the instrument.
  3. Find the manufacturer’s exact-model manual and compare it with any iFixit repair guide. Record model, revision, source date and unanswered questions. No exact-model guide or firmware retrofit is verified in this example.
  4. Explore Community Repair and ask an existing school, library or repair group about qualified supervision, age rules, costs and accessibility before attending. A group listing is not a booked session or vetted mentor.

Stop before hardware work: unplugging does not remove all stored electrical energy. Mains-powered circuitry, capacitors, CRTs, damaged batteries and unknown electrical faults need a qualified technician. A minor also needs appropriate adult supervision; an adult without technical competence is not a substitute. Do not discharge capacitors, solder, bypass protections or flash firmware as part of this reading exercise.

Keep your evidence: the external model/condition note, three component explanations, source links, one uncertainty and the next supervised learning step. A sketch, spoken note or text all work. This is a documentation exercise, not a tested repair, qualification or public submission.

Reference reading is free; tools, parts, travel and supervision can cost money. iFixit EDU describes institutional technical-writing partnerships; Loptr Lab’s eligibility and any kit, fee or staff support are unconfirmed.
