Art & Animation

Learn the fundamentals. Practice deliberately. Make original work. Keep evidence of your progress.

Learning sequence

  1. Drawing foundations. Practice confident lines, basic shapes, simple 3D forms, observation, and value. Resource: Proko Drawing Basics.
  2. Perspective and form. Draw boxes, cylinders, and objects from multiple viewpoints. Apply perspective to a room, prop, or environment.
  3. Gesture and figure construction. Begin with short gesture studies; develop movement, proportions, landmarks, and simplified forms. Resource: Figure Drawing Fundamentals.
  4. Character and visual design. Use silhouette, shape language, expression, proportion, and readable visual choices to design an original character.
  5. Choose a specialization. Explore illustration, concept art, storyboarding, animation, environment art, digital painting, or 3D character workflows according to the work goal.

Practice loop

  1. Choose one concept and watch a lesson or demonstration.
  2. Make several short studies rather than one polished copy.
  3. Compare your work with the reference and write down one specific observation.
  4. Repeat the exercise, changing one decision intentionally.
  5. Apply the skill to original work and save both versions as evidence.

Starter projects

  • Form study: draw five everyday objects using simple volumes, then show one object from three viewpoints.
  • Gesture sheet: create a set of short pose studies focused on movement, not detail.
  • Original character: produce silhouette exploration, construction sketch, expression/pose sheet, and a final design.
  • Environment or prop: make a perspective study and a finished original asset for a game or narrative project.

Portfolio evidence

For each project, retain the brief, references and their licenses where relevant, dated studies, iterations, a finished original piece, and a short reflection describing the skill practiced and the next improvement. Do not present copied course demonstrations as original portfolio work; clearly label studies and use them for learning.

Accessibility and flexible practice

  • Choose paper, pencil, tablet, software, or mixed media based on comfort and access—not a required brand.
  • Break practice into short sessions and allow rest or alternate input methods.
  • Use zoom, captions, adjustable contrast, reference rotation, stabilizers, or other available tools when useful.
  • Offer alternatives to timed gesture work, figure references, or content that is not suitable for a learner.

Cost and participation boundary

All Loptr Lab exercises on this page are free to access. External courses, software, hardware, and subscriptions are optional and may cost money. No paid resource is a prerequisite to contribute or demonstrate learning. This pathway is educational; it does not promise credentials, employment, funding, or individual instruction.
