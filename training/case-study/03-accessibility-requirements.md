# 3. Accessibility requirements

The participant describes functional needs without publishing diagnoses or private records. The plan includes readable scaling and contrast, reduced visual strain, resumable task state, low-memory-burden file handling, clear sequencing, alternate input where needed, and reliable ways to recover after interruption.

Each need is tied to a task and linked to the centralized [accessibility guidance](../accessibility/index.md), avoiding repeated or inconsistent accommodation text.

**Next:** [Translate requirements into a technology assessment](./04-technology-assessment.md).

## Adaptive creative work: a planning exercise

**Research question, not an integrated solution:** Can disabled participants contribute professionally to animation, motion-data editing, robotics simulation or teleoperation through adaptive interfaces when conventional capture equipment is inaccessible?

Choose one task: document an animation workflow, review interface access, or plan a retargeting test with material whose licence permits that use. Work with text only if preferred; no suit, disability disclosure, account or movement recording is required. Try keyboard/remapped input, an asynchronous review or smaller sessions according to the participant’s preferences. Do not decide access requirements for them.

Write this in your existing needs statement: **task; barrier; proposed adaptation; an alternative; source/date; observed result or “not tested”; rights limit; next decision**. Example: “Review animation timing; precise dragging is a barrier; try keyboard frame stepping; alternative is written time-code notes; no access test yet; use an original fictional sequence; ask whether the chosen controls work.” Done means another person can see what to test and what remains unknown. Keep medical and benefits evidence private.

### Choose the right support route

- **Workplace accommodation:** [EEOC guidance](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-reasonable-accommodation-and-undue-hardship-under-ada) explains effective accommodations, including equipment. Applicable duties depend on employment relationship, governing law, circumstances and undue hardship; the employer need not automatically buy a preferred product. [JAN](https://askjan.org/about-us/) offers free accommodation guidance, not funding or a legal determination.
- **Employment-related support:** [state VR policy](https://mn.gov/deed/job-seekers/disabilities/policies/) may support qualifying technology under agency rules, the employment plan and prior authorisation. Verify self-employment support separately. [State AT programmes](https://acl.gov/programs/assistive-technology/assistive-technology) may offer demonstrations or loans; they are not automatic ownership or a grant to the learner.
- **Medical coverage:** [Medicare’s artificial-limb guidance](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/artificial-eyes-limbs) concerns medically necessary ordered prostheses and individual costs. It does not establish coverage for a commercial motion-capture suit.
- **Commercial opportunity:** Xsens’s dated contest and PSYONIC’s products are independent examples, not job offers, agreed partnerships or equipment entitlements. Consult the [dated Made-Sick records and contest restrictions](https://made-sick.org/directory.html#resource-xsens-animation-contest-2026) before considering them. No Xsens–PSYONIC compatibility is verified. Contest data must not enter robotics/teleoperation or AI experiments outside its licence.

**Sources checked November 15, 2026.** Current rules and individual decisions must be verified with the responsible agency or qualified adviser. Training is not placement, benefits approval or independent legal review. Agree fair compensation and written terms for professional work; voluntary learning does not create a future pay promise. Preserve participant consent, attribution and control of movement data, including separate authorisation for secondary use or AI training.
