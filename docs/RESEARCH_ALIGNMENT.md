# Doctoral Research Alignment

## Research frame

**Working title:** *Organizational Leadership and Narrative World-Building in Independent Game Studio Production*

**Primary research question:** How do leadership and production structures in small, founder-led independent game studios shape—and get shaped by—the narrative worlds they build?

Veiled Dominion is the primary creative case in a longitudinal practice-research study at Loptr Lab. This repository is both a production environment and a traceable research artifact. Its role in the study does not override its role as the canonical four-player rules authority.

## Repository role

Loptr-Lab/veiled-dominion-engine contains the primary creative case: the canonical four-player, 14×14 cross-board design and the documentation that governs its rules and continuity.

Related repositories remain distinct:

- Loptr-Lab/duet-solo-hackathon is the accessibility artifact and an experimental two-player mechanics lab.
- Narrative canon, adaptations, separate reinterpretations, experiments, and historical/archive sources retain the boundaries defined in docs/CANON_AND_CONTINUITY.md.
- Code, mechanics, or conclusions do not transfer between artifacts without an explicit, documented decision.

## Research questions supported here

This repository provides evidence for the primary question and especially for:

- **RQ2:** How internal narrative frameworks—including lore, factions, mission language, and shared vocabulary—operate as organizational tools under changing teams, resource constraints, and uncertainty.
- **RQ3:** How founder-led creative practice evolves over multiple years and what that evolution reveals about sustainable leadership for solo and founder-driven studios.

## Practice-research loop

Repository work should preserve the study's analytic loop:

> Practice / Artifact → Question → Method → Evidence → Analysis → Finding → Contribution

Commits and pull requests are evidence of practice and decision-making, not findings by themselves. Research claims require analysis and contextualization outside the repository change that generated the evidence.

## Evidence practices

Relevant evidence may include:

- game and systems prototypes;
- design and world-building documents;
- production and architecture decisions;
- repository and version history;
- pitch and public-facing materials;
- studio and project documentation;
- reflexive accounts of leadership decisions;
- changes in priorities, constraints, and organizational structure.

Where practical:

1. Use focused branches and descriptive commits.
2. Explain the rationale, alternatives, and unresolved questions in pull requests or ADRs.
3. Label material as Canonical, Narrative canon, Adaptation, Experimental, Historical/archive, or Unresolved design decision.
4. Preserve superseded material when it has evidentiary value; archive or supersede it explicitly rather than silently rewriting provenance.
5. Separate observed evidence from later interpretation and research findings.

## Comparative and literature context

The wider doctoral project compares Loptr Lab with approximately two or three independent or founder-led studios and analyzes the practice alongside scholarship on leadership, creative industries, independent production, narrative/world-building, accessibility, and practice research.

This repository should document Loptr Lab's own practice accurately. It should not manufacture comparisons, findings, or scholarly conclusions that have not yet been established through the research method.
