## Purpose

This case teaches creators how to distinguish audience interaction, informal mutual promotion, exploratory strategy, and a contracted collaboration. It does not adjudicate private conduct, infer motives, or turn a real person into an antagonist.

The originating situation involved informal discussion of pre-release audience testing for an independent film, followed by public promotion of a creator interview and brand repositioning. The reusable lesson is how to vet a potential collaborator, recognize asymmetric professional support, set consent and access boundaries, and leave safely.

## Guiding principle

[TangSaying’s “Yin-Yang Is Not About ‘Balance’｜What the Original Actually Says”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XMcdIV3vkXs) is the interpretive prompt. This project uses a limited design reading: a healthy collaboration should not be evaluated by assuming both parties contribute identical things, but by examining the actual relationship between their contributions, obligations, power, and capacity to change.

That is a project interpretation, not a transcript, endorsement, or claim that the video supplies legal advice. Complementary work may be unequal in form; it must not become non-consensual, exploitative, coercive, or deliberately ambiguous.

## What a pre-marketing test is

A pre-marketing test is a small, time-bounded experiment conducted before a full rollout to evaluate audience, positioning, message, channel, or response. Before starting, write down:

- the hypothesis and success measure;
- the exact content and channels;
- names, likenesses, music, clips, and brands being referenced;
- who may approve, publish, edit, and remove material;
- whether the work is paid, reciprocal, speculative, or volunteered;
- the end date, data-retention period, and exit procedure;
- how attribution and later commercial use will be handled.

Public enthusiasm, private conversation, tags, reposts, or access to an audience do not themselves create a partnership.

## Vet before access

1. Verify identity, credits, references, and ownership or authority over the proposed project.
2. Ask for a written scope, responsible contact, timeline, compensation, and approval chain.
3. Review the person’s public promotions for privacy, consent, surveillance, harassment, and reputational risks.
4. Run a small reversible task before sharing source files, community access, customer information, or credentials.
5. Confirm how disagreement, personal boundaries, emergencies, and withdrawal will be handled.
6. Record conflicts of interest and whether personal, romantic, sexual, fan, or parasocial dynamics could impair professional consent.
7. Pause when someone responds to boundaries with pressure, retaliation, sexualization, threats, or escalating access demands.
8. Treat non-response, condescension, or unequal enthusiasm as information—not proof of malicious intent—and reduce investment accordingly.

## Discord and repository controls

A prospective collaborator receives a time-limited **Contributor Candidate** role with access only to onboarding, a sandbox channel, and the relevant project thread.

Do not grant a candidate:

- Administrator, moderator, billing, integration, or role-management permissions;
- private supporter, report, legal, safety, or payment channels;
- production secrets, customer records, addresses, analytics, or credentials;
- repository ownership, branch-protection control, deployment secrets, or store administration.

Promotion from candidate access requires a named sponsor, completed checklist, written scope, least-privilege review, and expiry date. Removal must revoke Discord roles, repository teams, tokens, shared drives, scheduling access, and third-party integrations.

## Contract exit protections

Use [Mike Monteiro’s “F*ck You, Pay Me”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jVkLVRt6c1U) as general creator-business education, not legal advice. Ask qualified counsel to draft:

- termination for convenience and termination for cause;
- a kill fee for completed and reserved work;
- payment milestones and late-payment consequences;
- ownership and license transfer only after payment;
- consent, anti-harassment, privacy, and non-retaliation terms;
- immediate suspension of access when safety is at issue;
- takedown, attribution, portfolio, confidentiality, and evidence-preservation rules;
- obligations that survive termination;
- a practical notice and dispute process that does not require unsafe direct contact.

A “kill clause” controls how the engagement ends. A “kill fee” states what remains payable after cancellation.

## Evidence and publication boundary

Keep private messages, platform reports, explicit material, residence information, unpublished files, and allegations in access-controlled storage. The public repository may retain neutral URLs, record identifiers, dates, and the creator-safety framework.

Do not describe a real person as dangerous, abusive, predatory, dishonest, or responsible for another person’s trauma without an appropriate evidentiary and legal basis. Fictionalize any playable adaptation and route identifiable publication through rights, privacy, and legal review.
