## 1.0 Concept Overview

A hyper-intellectual, "Gallery Edition" variant that strips away the gothic mythology and replaces it with the brutal, mechanical philosophies of 20th-century systems theory, economics, and biology.

This variant proves that the *Veiled Dominion* engine is a universal logic engine capable of simulating any conflict of ideologies. The board is no longer a cosmic classroom; it is a simulation of competing societal structures.

---

## 2.0 The Board: The Laboratory

* **Surface:** Frosted, anti-reflective white glass (opposite of obsidian).
* **The Grid:** Etched with precise, hairline silver rules. Visually resembles a massive iPad or laboratory whiteboard.
* **The Vibe:** Clinical, sterile, high-contrast. "Light" and "Void" are replaced by "Data" and "Noise."

## 3.0 The Four Factions (Asymmetric Mechanics)

### 3.1 Walter B. Cannon (The Biome)
* **Aesthetic:** Matte porcelain white and deep arterial red. Organic, smooth, cellular shapes.
* **The Mechanic ("Homeostasis"):** Cannon's pieces automatically adjust to threats. If a Cannon piece is attacked, it triggers a "Fight or Flight" response—it either automatically counter-attacks (Fight) or forces the attacking enemy piece back one square (Flight). Cannon wins via "Equilibrium" (Mercy Victory—completing the game without losing a single piece).

### 3.2 Arthur Coulter (The Cybernetic Loop)
* **Aesthetic:** Pieces are physically connected by fine, visible silver threads representing feedback loops.
* **The Mechanic ("Negative Feedback"):** When Coulter moves a piece, the silver threads "connect" to adjacent pieces. For one turn, those connected pieces cannot move *away* from Coulter's piece; they are mechanically pulled 1 square closer to correct the "systemic error" of their distance. Coulter wins by locking the enemy Leader in a web of threads (Checkmate).

### 3.3 Thorstein Veblen (The Leisure Class)
* **Aesthetic:** Pure, obnoxious luxury. Polished brass, gold leaf, heavy marble bases. Wildly out of place on the sterile white board.
* **The Mechanic ("Conspicuous Consumption"):** Veblen's pieces refuse to do "menial" work. A Veblen Rook *cannot* capture an enemy Pawn. If it moves to capture a Pawn, the move is illegal. Veblen pieces can only capture high-value targets (Rooks, Knights, Bishops, Queens). Pawns must be "outsourced" (dealt with by other players or avoided entirely).

### 3.4 Jacque Fresco (The Resource Economy)
* **Aesthetic:** 1960s retro-futurism. Seamless, translucent cyan/blue plastics. No sharp edges.
* **The Mechanic ("Abundance"):** Fresco does not believe in destruction (capturing). When a Fresco piece moves onto an enemy-occupied square, the enemy piece is not killed; it is "repurposed" and moved to a neutral "Resource Pool" at the edge of the board. Fresco can later spend a turn to place a repurposed piece back on the board as a neutral block that anyone can use for cover.

## 4.0 Rebirth & Death in the Systems Variant

* **Rebirth ➔ "The Exponential Curve" (Inflation/Collapse):** Instead of a glowing piece, it is a piece that visually multiplies (like a rapidly branching graph or fractal). The *Radius of Ruin* becomes "Hyper-Inflation." Any piece caught in the radius is "Devalued"—instead of losing abilities, they are forced to move *two* squares every turn, crashing wildly across the board out of control.
* **Death ➔ "The Great Reset" (Deflation/The Void):** Represented by the Musou Black piece.
