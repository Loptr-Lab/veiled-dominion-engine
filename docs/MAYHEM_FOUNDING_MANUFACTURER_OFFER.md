# Founding production distinction — SOURCE CODE apparel and Veiled Dominion artisan editions

**Correction to the earlier manufacturer brief · 2026-11-08.** The previous 50-copy factory-game/revenue-share example conflated two different products. Its $79 price, $38 factory unit cost, $1,300 invoice, 25% manufacturer share, and bonus math are **withdrawn as assumptions for Veiled Dominion**. They were hypothetical and are not evidence of a supplier offer. This file supersedes that mistaken proposal without adding a new commercial commitment.

## SOURCE CODE: clothing partnership (separate venture)

SOURCE CODE is the proposed apparel capsule. Its existing concept includes inclusive/unisex/androgynous and Big & Tall sizing, an LT block and technical garment specifications. A founding apparel drop may involve small-batch production and a manufacturing studio.

**Illustrative negotiating structure, not a quote or approved deal:** a garment production partner offers an itemized discount or deferred portion of first-run manufacturing costs, in return for a higher **capped and time-limited share of net receipts from the specifically contracted apparel run** or a **sales milestone bonus**. Use one incentive mechanism unless an explicit combined cap is agreed. Preserve Loptr Lab's designs, patterns, marks and IP; define maker rights and compensation. Require written unit costs, samples, grading/fit and accessibility checks, quality, returns, fulfillment, taxes, payout waterfall and a sunset date. No supplier or sale is confirmed.

Do not use Veiled Dominion's proposed 50 individually crafted editions as the SOURCE CODE garment quantity. The previously discussed apparel founding-drop range of 50–150 units **per style** is a separate planning assumption, not a production order. A garment revenue-share proposal belongs in the SOURCE CODE commercial plan, not the Gods of Mayhem sponsorship allocation.

## Veiled Dominion: first 50 physical games

The intended founding edition is **up to 50 predominantly handmade, individually crafted game sets**, rather than a 50-copy factory run. The appeal is attributable craftsmanship, care, material choices and provenance—an intimate, secret-society-inspired collector experience. **No paid secret-society membership, governance privilege or access to belonging** is created by buying a set. The free print-and-play game remains available.

Potential artisan arrangement (for exploration only):
- Invite individual makers to propose one or more numbered sets or components, with explicit scope, attribution preferences, approved materials, accessibility and safety standards, delivery schedule and compensation.
- Negotiate a fair per-piece commission, optional maker-recognized bonus or transparent share of actual receipts on that maker's work. Do not assume donated labor, blanket rights transfer or a standardized factory margin.
- Each set may differ aesthetically, but the board's canonical cross geometry, four-player piece counts, legibility, usability and playable rules must remain consistent. Record each maker, materials, edition number and care instructions with consent.
- Validate sample sets and actual labor/material/shipping/insurance/tax costs before establishing a retail price or advertising availability. Limit claims to the number actually commissioned and completed.
- Keep original game IP and each maker's independent creative rights explicitly distinguished; agree any reproductions, photographs, marketing permissions and future edition rights in writing.

## Partnership readiness boundaries

**Ready to discuss:** artisan interest, apparel manufacturer discovery, indicative quotations, accessible prototypes, and optional nonbinding collaboration structures.

**Not yet ready to claim:** an agreed supplier, negotiated revenue share, completed artisan inventory, confirmed $79 game retail price, sales bonus entitlement, net margin, funded apparel production, or licensed distribution.
